The Co-Intervention Imperative

Why Simple Pre/Post Averages Fail

In standard FP&A reporting, initiatives are frequently evaluated in isolation. However, with 3,844 stores simultaneously experiencing multiple interventions, an unadjusted comparison will double-count sales gains. For instance, a store receiving both Multi-Price Point ($3–$5 merchandise) and a Dedicated Cashier in February 2026 will show heightened throughput. Attributing 100% of the basket lift to MPP or 100% of transaction speed to DC creates a distorted causal picture. Multivariate Causal Synthetic Controls with Stratified Donors isolate the true marginal dollar lift per initiative with 0% unexplained residual.

Pure Controls Available: 2,000 Stores (22.2%)

One date provided by Dollar Tree upon completion of the overnight interior refresh. Store opens the next morning fully remodeled with zero comp closure days.

Discrete Step Events: Evaluated with zero customer discovery lead time using DT provided dates.

Why 6 Store Clusters Were Selected

Practical business and statistical reasons for choosing 6 clusters over 10 or more

1

Keeps Enough Control Stores

Using 10 clusters creates groups that are too small, leaving too few untreated stores to build good comparisons. Using 6 clusters provides deep, stable donor pools.

Deep comparison pools
2

Allows Regional Filtering

With 6 broad clusters, we can still filter control stores by geographic Supply Chain Zone to keep comparisons regionally accurate.

Regional accuracy
3

Similar Statistical Fit

Information criteria (BIC and Entropy) show almost identical separation between 6 and 10 clusters. Choosing 6 avoids unnecessary complexity.

Clean statistical fit
4

Scales to Future Initiatives

A clean 6-cluster model provides a consistent foundation across the 24+ upcoming initiatives planned for Phase 2.

Ready for 24+ initiatives

Econometric Stratification Framework

Test & Control Store Splitting Engine

Cluster-isolated donor pools, wave aggregation under Cluster × Zone × Month_Year, and the 4 non-negotiable control exclusion rules.

Zero Cross-Cluster Leakage
Core Pooling Philosophy No 1:1 Match

A Control Must Come From the Test Store's Own Cluster

A control must come from the test store's own cluster, so it sells like the test store. There is no one-to-one match. All eligible cluster stores form one pool, shared by all test stores that launch around the same time. Eliminating artificial pair matching prevents severe idiosyncratic variance and maximizes synthetic control donor weights.

Architecture: Shared Cluster Donor Pool
Wave Generation & Sizing N ≥ 25 Threshold

Waves Under Cluster × Zone by Launch Month

Waves are created under Cluster × Zone based on Initiative launch months (Month_Year). Adaptive Month Combination Rule: We combine up to 3 consecutive months if a single month does not have enough test stores (< 25 stores) to preserve statistical degrees of freedom.

Test Selection Rule: Legacy-to-MPP Transition Stores

The 4 Control Qualification Rules

Every candidate store in the cluster must pass all 4 rules to enter the shared control donor pool

4 of 4 Rules Enforced
Rule 1 (Isolation)
Target Initiative Active Only

Only target initiative active for test stores; zero initiatives for control stores during evaluation period.

Control requirement: Zero initiatives
Rule 2 (Clean Data)
Continuous Sales > $0

Continuous sales greater than zero (Sales > 0) across every single week.

Control requirement: No zero-sales weeks
Rule 3 (Neighbor Match)
Max 2–3 in 3 Miles

Limiting neighboring store activity to a maximum of 2 or 3 similar initiatives within 3 miles of the candidate store.

Control requirement: Neighbor activity cap
Rule 4 (Network Stability)
No Openings or Closures

No stores opened or closed within 3 miles during the period.

Control requirement: Stable 3-mile store network
