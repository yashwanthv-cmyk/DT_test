Why 6 Store Clusters Were Selected

Practical business and data reasons for choosing 6 store groups over 10 or more

1

Keeps Enough Comparison Stores

Using 10 clusters makes groups too small, leaving too few untreated stores for reliable comparisons. Using 6 clusters keeps healthy comparison pools.

Deep comparison pools
2

Allows Regional Filtering

With 6 broad clusters, we can still match stores within the same region so local market trends stay aligned.

Regional accuracy
3

Clean Group Separation

Model fit scores show almost identical separation between 6 and 10 clusters. Choosing 6 keeps groups clear and avoids over-splitting.

Clear & stable groups
4

Works for Future Rollouts

A steady 6-cluster setup gives us one consistent way to measure all upcoming store programs without rebuilding groups every time.

Ready for future initiatives

Rules for Picking Comparison Stores

Every comparison store in the same cluster must pass all 4 rules below

4 of 4 Rules Enforced
Rule 1 (Isolation)
Target Initiative Active Only

Only target initiative active for test stores; zero initiatives for control stores during evaluation period.

Control requirement: Zero initiatives
Rule 2 (Clean Data)
Continuous Sales > $0

Continuous sales greater than zero (Sales > 0) across every single week.

Control requirement: No zero-sales weeks
Rule 3
No Initiative in 3 Miles

Comparison stores must have zero neighboring store initiatives active within a 3-mile radius during the evaluation period.

Control requirement: Zero initiatives within 3 miles
Rule 4 (Network Stability)
No Openings or Closures

No stores opened or closed within 3 miles during the period.

Control requirement: Stable 3-mile store network
